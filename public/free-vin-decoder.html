What a VIN decoder can tell you — and what it can't

Those 17 characters on your Vehicle Identification Number aren't a random serial. Each position carries meaning, together spelling out how the car left the factory. Run them through a decoder and you'll get the model year, make, model, trim, body style, engine, fuel type, drivetrain, transmission and assembly plant. It's a fast way to confirm an online listing describes the actual car, double-check that a replacement part will fit, or catch a seller who has the trim wrong.

Here's the catch: the decoder can't touch the part of a car's past that affects its value. Factory specs are fixed the day the vehicle is built and never change, so they reveal nothing about crashes, a rolled-back odometer, a salvage or rebuilt title, or years of skipped maintenance. All of that is recorded later, over the vehicle's life, and it only shows up in a vehicle history report — never in the VIN alone.
